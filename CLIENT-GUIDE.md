## Your admin panel

This is where every application lands and where you can read and reply to emails.

**Go to:** https://www.coachedbyrudhra.com/admin

Enter your password and you're in. (Depending on how it was set up, you may also be asked for a username — if you only see a password box, that's normal and correct.)

**A few things to know:**

- You stay logged in for **8 hours**, then you'll be asked for the password again. This is deliberate — if you leave it open on a shared or lost device, access expires on its own.
- Use the **Log out** button, top right, when you're on a device that isn't yours.
- The page is hidden from Google. It will never show up in search results, and nobody can find it by browsing your site — there are no links to it anywhere.
- **Only share the password with people you trust completely.** There's one shared password rather than individual logins, so everyone who has it has full access, including the ability to delete applications permanently.

**If you ever need the password changed,** ask your developer. Changing it instantly logs out everyone who was signed in — which is exactly what you want if a phone goes missing or someone shouldn't have access any more.

## Checking new applications

Open the admin panel and you'll land on the **Applications** tab.

Applications appear **newest first**, one card each, the moment someone submits the form. You don't need to refresh anything or wait — if it's on the screen, it's saved.

Each card shows:

- **Their name**, at the top
- **Their phone number**, which you can tap to call directly from your phone
- **The date and time** they applied (Indian time)
- **All of their answers** — schedule, why now, frustration level out of 10, what it would mean to them, goal, activity level, injuries, where they'll train, diet, weekly commitment, and their answer on investment

Anything they left blank shows as a dash.

### You'll also get an email

Every application is emailed to you straight away, so you don't have to keep checking the panel. That email contains all their answers, plus two shortcuts: **tap the phone number to call**, or tap the green **Message on WhatsApp** button to open a chat with them directly.

The application is saved to the panel *before* the email is sent — so even if an email ever fails to arrive, **the application is never lost**. The panel is always the reliable record.

### Deleting an application

There's a delete button on each card. It asks you to confirm first, and names the person so you can't delete the wrong one by mistake.

> **This is permanent.** A deleted application cannot be recovered by you, your developer, or anyone else. If you're unsure, leave it — there's no cost to keeping it.

## Replying to emails

The **Received emails** tab shows messages sent to your website addresses — for example, anyone writing to `support@coachedbyrudhra.com`.

For each message you'll see who sent it, which of your addresses they wrote to, when it arrived, and the message itself.

**To reply,** type into the box beneath the message and send. Your reply:

- Comes **from your own domain**, not a personal address — so it looks professional
- Lands **in the same conversation thread** in their inbox, exactly as a normal email reply would
- Gets `Re:` added to the subject automatically

The person receives an ordinary email. Nothing about it looks automated or reveals that it was sent from a web panel.

### "Remove" hides an email — it doesn't delete it

The remove button clears a message out of your panel to reduce clutter. **The email itself still exists** in your email provider's records. Think of it as archiving, not deleting.

One thing to be aware of: **you can't un-remove a message from the panel.** If you remove something and need it back, your developer can restore it. It's not lost, just out of sight.

## What visitors experience

Your homepage isn't a long page people scroll. It's a guided journey, designed so someone earns interest before being asked for anything.

**1. They arrive** at your headline over your training footage.

**2. They can take a free assessment** — six quick taps. No email, no signup, nothing to fill in. It asks their sex, goals, age range, height, weight, and how many days a week they can train.

**3. They get a personalised result.** The site estimates their body composition and shows what's realistically achievable in 12 weeks.

This part was built carefully and conservatively, because it makes a promise on your behalf:

- Weight-loss projections use a **sustainable rate**, and the site will never project someone below a healthy weight
- If someone is already at a healthy weight, it says so honestly — *"You're already at a healthy weight. So we build strength, not a deficit."*
- If their goals aren't weight-related at all, it refuses to invent a number — *"Your win here isn't on the scale."*
- It always shows a **range**, never a single guaranteed figure, and labels the estimate as an estimate

You can stand behind every number it shows.

**4. They go through your story** — eight screens covering who you are, why this works, the program, who it's for, what changes, real client results, FAQs, and finally an invitation to apply or message you on WhatsApp.

Anyone who'd rather skip straight to the information can, from the first screen.

> **Note:** assessment answers are **not saved anywhere**. They're a tool to build interest, not a record. If someone takes the assessment but doesn't fill in the application, you won't have their details.

## Getting found on Google

### What's been done

Your homepage works beautifully for people, but Google reads a page differently — it couldn't see past your opening screen to any of your story. So your content now also lives on proper pages that Google can read in full:

- **Coaching & Pricing** — the program, what makes it different, who it's for, what changes, and your pricing
- **About Rudhra** — your story and approach
- **FAQ** — your five most common questions
- **Client Results** — your transformations

These are linked from the bottom of every page, so visitors and Google can both find them.

On top of that, your site tells Google specifically what your business is: an online personal training and nutrition service, based in India, priced from ₹10,000, run by you. Your FAQs are marked up so Google can display those questions and answers **directly in search results**. Your client photos are submitted for image search. Sharing any page on WhatsApp, Instagram or LinkedIn shows a branded preview card rather than a bare link.

The site was also made faster — your background videos no longer download until after the page is readable, which matters because Google factors loading speed into rankings.

### What you need to do

**One thing, and it does need to be you** — it requires your Google account:

1. Go to **[Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console)** and sign in
2. Add `www.coachedbyrudhra.com` as a property (your developer can help verify ownership)
3. Under **Sitemaps**, submit: `sitemap.xml`

This tells Google your pages exist. Without it, Google will find them eventually on its own, but far more slowly.

Search Console is also where you'll see which searches bring people to your site — genuinely useful for knowing what your audience is actually looking for.

### Setting expectations honestly

SEO is slow. A new site typically takes **several weeks to a few months** to appear meaningfully in search results, and competing for broad terms like "online personal trainer" takes longer still. What's been built is a correct, solid foundation — but it isn't an overnight switch, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

The fastest wins available to you:

- **Link your site from your Instagram bio.** Real visitors from social media are the strongest early signal you can send Google.
- **Ask happy clients for Google reviews** once you have a Google Business Profile.
- **If you ever run in-person sessions from a fixed location**, tell your developer — a real business address unlocks local search results ("personal trainer near me"), which currently isn't possible because you're online-only.

## Things worth knowing

Honest notes about how the panel behaves, so nothing catches you out.

**Every application shows a "NEW" tag.** Including ones you've already called. It isn't a status you can change or tick off — it's a label on every card. To track who you've dealt with, work from the date, or use your own notes.

**You can see your most recent 200 applications.** More than enough for a long while, but worth knowing there's a limit, and there's no search box yet.

**Deleting is permanent.** Worth repeating, because it's the only action on the panel that can't be undone.

**Nothing marks an application as "contacted".** If you'd like a way to tick applications off, or search them, or export them to a spreadsheet — that's a reasonable feature to ask your developer for. It doesn't exist today.

## If something looks wrong

The panel tells you plainly when something isn't working. Here's what the messages mean.

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "No applications yet" | Nothing has been submitted. | Nothing — this is normal on a quiet week. |
| "Admin password is not configured yet" | The password isn't set on the server. | Contact your developer. You can't fix this yourself. |
| "Incorrect password" | Wrong password, or it was changed. | Try again; then contact your developer. |
| Anything mentioning **Firestore** or **credentials** | The panel can't reach the database. | Contact your developer. **Your applications are safe** — this is a connection problem, not lost data. |
| Anything mentioning **Resend** | The panel can't read your emails. | Contact your developer. Your email itself still works normally; check your regular inbox meanwhile. |

**A good rule:** if a message names a technical service you don't recognise, it's not something you've done wrong and not something you can fix. Screenshot it and send it on.

### Worried an application was lost?

It almost certainly wasn't. Applications are saved to the database *before* any email goes out, so an email that doesn't arrive doesn't mean a lost lead. **Check the admin panel** — that's the reliable record. If it's in the panel, you have it.
